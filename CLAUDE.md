# Trig Documentation Style Guide

This guide ensures all documentation speaks in Trig's voice and follows the Trig Design System. When writing or editing docs, follow these principles.

## Design System

This documentation uses the Trig Design System. The color palette uses muted, warm tones:

### Color Palette
- **Primary blue**: #5A7A99 (links, buttons, interactive elements)
- **Action color**: #8F8871 (secondary interactive elements)
- **Body text**: #2D3339 (warm charcoal)
- **Background**: #f7f7f7 (warm off-white)

### Key Principles
- Muted, sophisticated colors - no harsh or vibrant tones
- Warm undertones throughout (greys have warm, not cool, undertones)
- High contrast for readability
- Consistent with brand identity

## Voice Principles

### Sophistication Made Simple
We make powerful things feel effortless. Explain complex concepts without dumbing them down. The reader should feel capable, not confused.

### The Future is Now
AI is good news. It lets you do things you couldn't do before. We're not selling inevitable obsolescence - we're opening new possibilities. Keep things tangible and real, not sci-fi.

### Ordinary Made Extraordinary
We take everyday tasks and make them remarkable. Show how Trig transforms routine work into something powerful.

## Guiding Values

1. **Simple** - 100% clarity, as briefly as possible. Write headlines as if it's illegal to write subcopy.
2. **Authentic** - We're different because we believe in a different way. Not different for disruption's sake.
3. **Honest** - Open and straightforward. This builds trust and genuine impact.

## How We Write

### We're Human
- Use everyday words that real people use
- Contractions are good: we're, you're, can't, doesn't, won't
- Bend grammar rules when it sounds natural (but don't break them)
- No B2B jargon or stiff corporate speak

### We're Concise
- Aim for 100% clarity in as few words as possible
- Inspire confidence, not confusion
- Universal simplicity, max transparency

### We're Energetic
- Pacey, direct, full of infectious enthusiasm
- Trig has immense ambition and dramatic impact
- Show that energy in the writing

### We're Modern
- Light-hearted when it's needed, not just when expected
- A well-chosen word beats five mediocre ones
- Make the experience richer in as few words as possible

## In-Product Copy Rules

These are rules, not guidelines.

### Text Formatting
| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| Use contractions: we're, you're, can't | We are, you are, cannot |
| Use numerals: 10 | Spell out: ten |
| Sentence case for headings | Title Case For Headings |
| Email (no hyphen) | E-mail |
| Dropdown (one word) | Drop-down |
| Login (adjective), Log in (verb) | Mix these up |

### Word Choices
| Use | Instead of |
|---|---|
| View | See |
| Change (switching options) | Edit (when switching) |
| Edit (modifying something) | Change (when modifying) |
| Choose (decisions between valid options) | Select (when choosing) |
| Select (picking from a list where one is true) | Choose (when selecting) |
| For example, like | E.g. |
| and | & (ampersand) |
| Words | Emojis |

### Numbers and Dates
- Use numerals: There are 9 locations
- Spell out numbers starting sentences: Nine locations help us...
- Use k, m, bn for large numbers when space is tight: 5k, 2m
- Dates: 1 March 2023 (not 1st March)
- Times: 8:00 am, 7:00-11:30 pm
- Use % symbol, not "percent"
- Use "From X to Y" or "Between X and Y" rather than dashes for ranges

### Punctuation
- Full stops: Use them. Break up long sentences.
- No full stops in headings
- Bullet points: No full stops at the end
- Oxford comma: Only when needed for clarity
- Em dash (—) with spaces for emphasis or interruption
- Ellipses: No spaces before or after...like this
- Exclamation marks: Rarely. One at a time if you must.

### Voice and Tone
- Active voice: "Jon broke the sauna" not "The sauna got broken by Jon"
- Second person (you, your) when addressing the user
- Be direct and specific
- Lead with the action you want them to take
- Offer help if they're stuck

### Email Best Practices
- Plain text emails work best
- They look personal and get higher engagement
- Avoid branded HTML templates - they scream "marketing automation"
- Sign off with a real name
- Keep it short: 3-5 sentences for simple actions

### What to Avoid
- Acronyms unless commonly used (PDF is fine, don't spell it out)
- Brackets unless referencing abbreviations or data
- Slang that doesn't work in B2B context
- "Etc." or "and more" - give examples instead
- Formal openers like "Dear"
- Generic closings like "The TeamFlow Team"
