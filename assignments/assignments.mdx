---
title: "Assignments"
description: "Work Trig does across a Book's accounts, once or on a schedule"
---

An **assignment** is a piece of work Trig does across a Book's accounts. For example: "Every Monday, check accounts renewing in the next 60 days for open urgent tickets, and flag them for the account owner."

## How an assignment comes together

1. **Choose the Book and when it runs.** Start with **Once** while you are testing.
2. **Describe the work.** Say what to look at, what counts as a problem and what to do about it. The more specific you are, the better the result. Name the threshold, such as "more than three urgent tickets", and the action, such as "draft an email" or "post in Slack".
3. **Click Plan it out.** The plan shows what you'll get, what it reads and what it does. If it needs a toolkit you can't reach, it says which one.
4. **Read the plan.** Check it reads the source you expected, runs on the Book you chose, uses your values and thresholds, and ends the way you asked. If it is wrong, change the description and plan it again.
5. **Approve the plan.** Approving the plan makes the assignment live.

## When it runs

- **On a schedule:** runs whether or not anything changed.
- **When something changes:** runs for each account that starts meeting a condition.
- **Once:** runs one time, as soon as you start it.

## What it produces

Each run writes a page for each account it looked at. You choose how people hear about a new result, in the app or in Slack. After each account's result lands, the assignment can take actions, such as drafting an email or posting a message. Trig can write each message, or you can write them. Nothing an assignment drafts goes to a customer until someone on your team approves it.

## Status

An assignment is a draft until you approve its plan. After that it is active, and you can pause it. A one-time assignment is completed after it runs.

## Common questions

- **Why is the plan wrong?** The description left something out, such as the source to read or the threshold to use. Add it and plan again.
- **Whose connections does it use?** The connections of the person who created it.
