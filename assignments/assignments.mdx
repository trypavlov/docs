---
title: "Assignments"
description: "Work Trig does across a Book's accounts, once or on a schedule"
---

An **assignment** is a piece of work Trig does across a Book's accounts. For example: "Every Monday, check accounts renewing in the next 60 days for open urgent tickets, and flag them for the account owner."

## How an assignment comes together

1. **Describe the work.** Say what to look at, what counts as a problem and what to do about it. The more specific you are, the better the result. Name the threshold, such as "more than three urgent tickets", and the action, such as "draft an email" or "post in Slack".
2. **Trig plans it.** The plan shows what you'll get, what it reads and what it does. If it needs a toolkit you haven't connected, it says which one.
3. **Try it on a few accounts.** Trig runs it on some trial accounts and writes their pages. Read them and change the description until they look right.
4. **Start it.** The assignment goes live on its schedule.

## When it runs

- **On a schedule:** runs whether or not anything changed.
- **When something changes:** runs for each account that starts meeting a condition.
- **Once:** runs one time, as soon as you start it.

## What it produces

Each run writes a page for each account it looked at. You choose how people hear about a new result, in the app or in Slack. After each account's result lands, the assignment can take actions, such as drafting an email or posting a message. Trig can write each message, or you can write them. ## Status

An assignment is a draft until you start it. After that it is active, and you can pause it. A one-time assignment is completed after it runs.

## Common questions

- **Why can't I start my draft?** It is still planning, it needs a toolkit that isn't connected, or its trial hasn't run. The draft says which.
- **Whose connections does it use?** The connections of the person who created it.
